Part 1

Alex asks, you answer out loud

“What is the weather usually like where you live?”

The examiner asks about familiar topics, listens to the end of your answer and moves on as an examiner would. The clock runs to the exam’s timing, so you get used to answering at length without notes.

  • Part 1, about 4 min
  • Live voice
  • No typing

Part 2

The task card, one minute to prepare

“Describe a story you read that you later discussed with someone.”

You get a task card with the points to cover, one minute to plan, then up to two minutes to speak. Alex stops you at two minutes and asks a rounding-off question, as on the day.

  • 1 min to prepare
  • 2 min to speak

Your report

An estimated band for each criterion

After Part 3 you see an estimated overall band and one for each of the four criteria: Fluency and coherence, Lexical resource, Grammatical range and accuracy, and Pronunciation. Each one points to the words you said.

AI practice estimate. Not an official IELTS result.

Fixes

Feedback that quotes your own words

Each fix shows your line, a better version and one reason in plain English. You can play back your answer to hear where it went wrong.

Say it again

Say the better line, then compare

Tap Say it again, answer the same question with the better line, and see what changed between your first answer and the new one. Reading a correction is a start; saying it is what makes it stick.

Today

Which answer is the Band 7?

Two answers to the same Part 1 question, written for this page. Tap the one you think the public descriptors rate higher.

“Do you prefer planning a trip carefully or making decisions as you go?”

When I was go to Italy last year

When I went to Italy last year

One past tense is enough. Grammatical range and accuracy.

so I was feel relax

which meant I could actually relax

A result clause joins the two ideas and keeps the tense. Fluency and coherence.

he like to decide in the moment

he’d rather decide on the day

Subject and verb agree, and a natural phrase replaces a literal one. Lexical resource.

Before you start

Is BandNine an official IELTS test?

No. BandNine is a practice app. Every band it shows is an AI practice estimate, not an official IELTS result. Only the test you take at an IELTS centre gives you a band you can use.

Who is Alex?

Alex is the examiner in the app: an AI voice that runs the Speaking test in the same order as the real one. No person listens to your answers while you speak.

How are the bands estimated?

After the test, your transcript and the recording of your answers are scored against the four Speaking criteria: Fluency and coherence, Lexical resource, Grammatical range and accuracy, and Pronunciation. Each estimate comes with the words from your answers that support it. If there is not enough evidence for a criterion, the report says so instead of guessing.

Can I practise one part at a time?

Yes. You can take Part 1, Part 2 or Part 3 on its own, a full mock test of all three parts, or a two-minute quick drill of Part 1 questions on one topic.

What does it cost?

The app is free to download and every new account gets 17 credits, enough for one full mock test with its report. After that you get 2 free credits every Monday. More practice uses credit packs or BandNine Plus, weekly or monthly. The cost of each test is shown before you start.

What happens if the connection drops during a test?

If a test fails because of a fault on our side, the credits for it go back to your balance. If you leave early, you pay only for the parts you started, and the app shows that amount before you leave.

Do I need an account?

The app creates an anonymous account the first time you open it, so you can start without an email address. Link Apple or Google if you want to keep your history on a new phone.

How do I delete my data?

Open the You tab and tap Delete account. Your recordings, transcripts, band estimates and account are removed straight away. The delete-account page explains how to ask by email instead.
